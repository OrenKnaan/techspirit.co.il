Building a Website for a Spiritual Business: 5 Things I Learned from Projects

June 28, 20265 min readOren Knaan

Building websites for spiritual businesses is different from others. I know that because I've built plenty of both. Here are the 5 things I learned.

Spirituality is a worldview that sees intelligence in everything: in relationships, in trust, and in how things fall into place when you listen to them. It isn't an abstract concept. It's a way of acting in the world.

When I build a website for a conscious person, a coach, a facilitator, this is the framework I work within. Not only "what looks good" and "what converts," but "what's real" and "what will correctly reflect the person behind the business."

5 things that make a spiritual business website effective

Principle 1

The content has to come from real experience

When a therapist writes "15 years of experience," it's supposed to mean something. But when they write "I've seen people who thought they'd never change, and after three months together they no longer recognize themselves," that's something else entirely. Google looks for E-E-A-T, real experience. So do your clients.

Principle 2

The design should reflect the energy, not "look professional"

There's a difference between a website that looks professional and one that feels right. A therapist who works with mind-body healing doesn't need a website that looks like a law firm. The colors, the fonts, the spacing between elements, are all part of the message.

Principle 3

Building trust matters more than conversion tricks

Yes, you want people to get in touch. But the way there is different. A client looking for guidance or change needs a deep sense of safety. A website with a "three-hour sale" timer doesn't build that safety. A website with real testimonials and a real photo of the therapist does. Choosing the builder who understands this is a critical step.

Principle 4

The client is looking for resonance, not a list of features

Someone looking for an anxiety therapist isn't looking for "evidence-based methodologies." They're looking for someone who will make them feel understood. Your website needs to speak the language they speak, not the language of the professional literature. How to write that kind of content - there's a method.

Principle 5

An honest website is also a website that attracts the right clients

When the website reflects who you are correctly, people who aren't a fit don't reach out. It sounds like a drawback but it's an advantage. Fewer conversations that go nowhere. More clients who truly want what you offer.

Questions and answers about building a spiritual business website

What is a website for a spiritual business?

A website for conscious people, coaches, facilitators, art, yoga and meditation teachers is different from an ordinary business website because the client is also looking for human connection and deep trust.

The website needs not only to explain what's offered, but also to feel right, safe, and real. Someone arriving at such a website isn't looking for a price tag, they're looking for a sense of security. The website is the first impression, and often it's what decides whether they'll get in touch.

How much does it cost to build a spiritual business website?

A website coded with AI costs ₪3,000-7,000, with custom design and built-in SEO ₪7,000-18,000.

The gap comes from the level of design, the number of pages, and what's included in the offer. A website coded with AI gives full flexibility without the limits of ready-made platforms. Before comparing offers, make sure you're comparing the same product.

What should a therapist's website include?

A home page that explains who the audience is and what the problem is, a personal and real About page, a services page, client testimonials, and a simple way to get in touch.

Real testimonials with a name, not anonymous ones, are one of the most significant factors in conversion. People believe other people more than marketing texts. Three real testimonials are better than twenty made-up ones.

Does a therapist need a blog?

Yes, if you have time to write, because a blog is the most effective tool for long-term SEO.

Every article that answers a question you're asked again and again is an opportunity to rank on Google. Even 2-3 articles a month are enough. An article you wrote in 2024 can keep bringing clients in 2027 with no additional investment.

What's the difference between a coach's website and a therapist's website?

Usually a difference in phrasing: a coach talks about goals and change, a therapist talks about healing and process.

Both need a website that expresses trust and personality. A coach can use the language of "results" and "achievements." A therapist will usually talk about "process" and "safety." When the website reflects the right language, the right client recognizes themselves.

How do you attract clients through the website?

SEO on keywords relevant to the location and field, an up-to-date Google Business Profile, content that answers questions, and a clear CTA.

The most effective combination: a blog article that ranks for a question clients ask leads to a service page with testimonials, which leads to a direct WhatsApp. Each stage warms the client up before they reach the point of getting in touch.

What is a Google Business Profile?

The profile on Google Maps, free, and when someone searches 'NVC practitioner near me' your profile appears.

It's just as important as the website for the local market. A profile with 10 positive reviews and up-to-date photos brings local clients who wouldn't have found you any other way. Setting it up takes an hour and is worth every moment.

Is a yoga website different from a therapist's website?

A little. A yoga website usually includes a class schedule, a therapist emphasizes a first consultation session; both need a design that conveys calm and trust.

A yoga website also needs to explain what's offered to people who aren't yet familiar with the specific style, Nidra yoga versus Ashtanga versus Yin. People who don't know the differences need a short explanation.

What's the difference between a web designer and a developer?

A designer focuses on the visual experience: how it looks and how it feels. A developer focuses on code and function: what works and what's secure.

Most small businesses need someone who does both. When there's a split between a designer and a developer, the website usually comes out beautiful but slow, or fast but not feeling right. For most small businesses, a website builder who knows both design and technology is the right choice.

Is it worth using AI images?

Real photos of the business owner work much better, because people want to know who they're going to meet.

AI images can look uniform and artificial. For most therapy businesses, a professional photo shoot once a year is better than any sophisticated AI image library. People who see a real photo of you feel they already know you.

What is responsive design?

Design that fits every screen size, and about 70% of browsing today is from a mobile device.

A website that isn't adapted loses clients and gets a low score from Google. To check: open your website on your phone and do everything a visitor might do. If something is hard, it's hard for the visitor too.

How long does it take to get clients from a new website?

In a non-competitive market one to two months, in a competitive market 6-12 months of consistent SEO.

It depends on SEO and competition. In a small town with few competitors, results come faster. In big cities with many therapists, it takes more time and quality content. Consistent investment over the first year usually yields good results.

Is it important for the website to load fast?

Yes, 53% leave a website that takes more than 3 seconds, and speed is also a Google ranking factor.

Heavy images, unnecessary plugins, and slow hosting are the common causes of slowness. A tool like Google's PageSpeed Insights can diagnose exactly what's slowing your website down, and it's free.

What is White Label in website building?

When an agency builds websites for a third party's clients under the third party's brand; not relevant for most small business owners.

What is relevant to know: make sure the website built for you belongs to you and not the builder, so you can take it if you decide to work with someone else. It's a question worth asking every website builder before you sign.
