How to Choose a Website Builder That Fits You

June 2, 20265 min readOren Knaan

The most common problem usually comes from a mismatch between your needs and the professional strengths of the website builder.

This is how I think about it: trying to fuel a gasoline engine with diesel. Nothing is wrong with diesel. Nothing is wrong with the engine. But together, they don't work.

When a client comes to me and says "I tried two builders before, and it didn't work out," I usually don't think those builders were bad. I ask what they did and why it didn't work.

Often the answer is: they did what they know how to do. But it wasn't what the client needed.

What's the difference between one website builder and another

Some developers are excellent at technology but have no feel for design. Some designers produce beautiful websites that load slowly. Some cheap freelancers will give you a template with your name on it and call it a website. Some expensive agencies will pass you between people until you lose yourself.

And sometimes, what you need is someone who understands your field well enough to know what your clients are looking for.

In the fields of therapy and spirituality, there's a special language. There's trust to be built. There's a way your clients search and what moves them to reach out. A builder who doesn't know this will make you a website that looks professional but doesn't feel right. I wrote separately about ethical marketing that suits the field.

Questions worth asking before you decide

  • Do you have experience with businesses in my field? Look at the portfolio and ask.
  • Who writes the content? You? Me? What's included in the package?
  • What happens after the website goes live? Who updates, who maintains, who do you contact when there's a problem?
  • Who owns the code? It's important to make sure that if one day you want to switch, you can take your website with you.
  • What's included in the SEO? Were the headings done properly, the meta descriptions, the speed? Or is the SEO just "the number of keywords"?

No one's to blame, it's about fit

A website builder who gave you a template for 800 shekels isn't necessarily bad. They're just not the right fit if you need SEO, a unique design, and someone who understands your client base. Not everyone needs the same thing. Before you search, there are questions worth asking first.

Questions and answers about choosing a website builder

How do I choose a website builder for my business?

Look for someone who has worked with businesses similar to yours, look at their portfolio, and check that communication feels comfortable.

Price is a factor, but not the first one. The best way to check: read articles the builder has written, see how they explain things, and notice whether their language feels familiar. Someone who explains well in writing usually explains well during a project too.

How much does building a WordPress website cost in Israel?

A basic website runs 2,000-4,000 shekels, a professional one with custom design and SEO 5,000-12,000 shekels, and with a store 10,000 shekels and up.

Always ask what's included: SEO? Content writing? Support afterwards? Training on managing it? Each of these is worth money and affects the price. Two price quotes with identical totals can include completely different things.

What's the difference between a freelancer and an agency?

Freelancer: personal work, flexibility, lower price. Agency: a team, defined processes, ongoing support.

For small businesses, a focused freelancer is usually better. The danger with an agency: you work with whoever is available, not necessarily with someone who understands your field. The danger with a freelancer: if they disappear, there's no one to continue.

What should I ask a website builder before deciding?

Experience with similar businesses? Who writes the content? How long will it take? Who maintains it afterwards? Annual maintenance cost? Is basic SEO included?

Also ask: who owns the code? It's a question most people don't ask and regret later. If the builder holds the code, you're dependent on them forever. The code has to be yours.

What is WordPress?

A content management system that powers about 43% of the world's websites - flexible, excellent SEO, and thousands of plugins.

A business owner can manage content on their own after a short training. The reason most website builders in Israel work with it is that it allows full flexibility without writing code. Almost every feature you can think of exists as a plugin.

What's the difference between WordPress and Wix?

Wix is easy to manage on your own; WordPress gives full control and deeper SEO.

For businesses that want to grow and need serious SEO, WordPress is better. Wix suits those who want to manage things themselves and don't need full flexibility. The significant difference: Wix doesn't allow access to the code, WordPress does.

Is it worth going with the cheapest option?

Usually not, because a website that doesn't bring clients isn't an investment, it's an expense.

A very cheap freelancer may give you a website that looks like everyone else's, with poorly tuned SEO, no training on managing it, and no support afterwards. Ask what's included, not just what it costs. Sometimes the difference between 3,000 and 6,000 shekels is SEO and support that change everything.

What is Elementor?

A plugin for building WordPress websites visually, drag and drop, that allows flexible design without code.

It's very common in Israel, and it's the tool I use. The Pro version includes ready-made templates, advanced widgets, and speed-improvement capabilities. Elementor makes it possible to build beautiful, flexible websites in a relatively short time.

How long does it take to build a website?

A basic website takes one to two weeks, a custom one with original design 3-6 weeks; the factor that slows things down most is the content the client needs to provide.

Clients who arrive with texts and images ready get their website weeks before those who wait to finish writing. If you're planning to build a website, it's worth starting to gather content now and not waiting for the first meeting.

What is website management and who does it?

WordPress updates, backups, adding content, fixing issues - some people manage it themselves and others pay for a maintenance package.

Ask about it before you sign. A monthly maintenance package usually costs 300-800 shekels and covers updates, backups and support. If you don't plan to manage it yourself, make sure there's a clear agreement on what happens after the website goes live.

What's the difference between UX and UI?

UI is the visual look: colors, fonts, buttons. UX is the experience of using it: is it easy to find what you're looking for?

A good website needs both. A beautiful website with poor UX doesn't bring clients, because people get confused and leave before reaching out. Ask the builder: "How does a client get from the home page to getting in touch?" A clear answer points to UX thinking.

Do I need a contract with a website builder?

Yes. A contract defines what's included, timelines, payment terms, ownership of the code, and what happens if there are changes.

It protects both sides. Without a contract, when there are disagreements it's unclear what was promised and what wasn't. Even a summary by email is better than a phone call alone. A designer or developer who refuses to sign a contract raises a warning flag, and it's worth reconsidering working with them.

What is website hosting and who takes care of it?

The server the website sits on, and what matters is knowing who holds the hosting account - the builder or you.

If the builder holds the hosting account and you part ways, you may end up dependent on them for access to your own website. It's always better for the account to be in your name. A builder can set up hosting for you and manage it, but the ownership should be yours.

What should I do if I'm not happy with the website?

First, explain clearly what isn't working and what the expectations were, and if there's a contract, refer to it.

If there's no agreement, you can turn to another builder with the existing code. That's why it's important that the code belongs to you and not the builder. A direct conversation is always better than bad feelings. Good website builders prefer to fix things and keep a satisfied client over a client who leaves angry.
