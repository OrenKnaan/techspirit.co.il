Building an Online Presence: What I Learned from Reality Creation

June 19, 20265 min readOren Knaan

Many business owners build an online presence out of fear: "I have to have a website, everyone already has one." That's not the best foundation for something that's supposed to represent you.

For years I tried to understand what makes some people build an online presence that works while others are left with no results. I built many websites and saw both kinds up close.

One difference stands out again and again: intention. Not in a spiritual or mystical sense, but in the most practical sense there is. People who come to a project with clarity about who they are and what they want others to know about them get a website that works. People who come with an approach of "make me a website like X's" will usually get a website that looks like X but doesn't feel like them.

The connection to reality creation

There's a concept in reality creation that I like: the difference between "intention" and "wanting." When you want something, you act from a feeling of lack. When you have an intention, you're already acting from knowing it's happening.

Building a website that comes from intention looks like this:

  • You have clarity about the client you want to help.
  • You have something real to say, and not just "years of experience" to display.
  • The content is written from a real wish to give value, and not from fear that someone won't buy.

Building a website out of fear, on the other hand, looks like this: "Everyone already has one, so I have to as well. Make me something that looks professional." A website that grows from this approach usually feels generic, just like all the rest.

What this means in practice

Before you start building, there are a few questions worth asking ourselves:

  • Who is the specific person I most want to help?
  • What are they searching for on Google before they reach me?
  • What can I offer them that others can't?

When we have clear answers to these questions, it's much easier to write and design the website. And when the website reflects our truth, it also produces results. Here are 4 key questions worth asking before you start.

On attention and SEO

There's a principle in reality creation that says: what you give attention to grows. In SEO it works exactly like that in the most tangible way. What you write about, the topics you add to the website, the questions you answer - all of these keep growing on Google. Consistency simply works.

Your digital presence: an inseparable part of who you are

In the fields of therapy and spirituality, people don't just buy a service - they choose a person. Your digital presence - your website, the words you wrote, the tone you sound in - is the first impression you leave. That's why an independent website is far better than a presence on social networks alone.

A website that reflects you honestly and authentically is a website that will draw to you exactly the right clients - those you have a real connection with, and not only those looking for the cheapest price.

Questions and answers about a business's online presence

What is an online presence for a business?

Everything people find when they search for you online: your website, your Google business profile, social networks and articles, which together create the overall picture of you before the first contact.

A strong online presence means that when someone searches for the service you offer, they find you, read about you, and feel they already know you before they pick up the phone. That significantly shortens the decision process and brings you clients who are already ready to work with you.

How do you build an online presence for a business?

Start with the foundation: a website and a Google Business Profile, add valuable content on an ongoing basis, and grow your exposure through organic promotion (SEO).

There are no shortcuts. The most common mistake is trying to be present on every platform at once. It's better to focus first on a strong, stable foundation, and only after it starts bringing results, expand to additional channels. A great website and Google Business Profile alone can bring a steady stream of clients if they were built right.

How long does it take to build a presence online?

Building a basic presence takes about one to two months, a presence that yields meaningful SEO results takes about 6 to 12 months, and a strong presence based on quality content accompanies the business for years.

Organic promotion (SEO) is a long-term investment, not a quick magic fix. Someone who starts today will see real results in about half a year, and enjoy the fruits of them for years to come. That's exactly why the best time to start is now.

What's the difference between local SEO and general SEO?

Local promotion targets searches that include a geographic location (like 'NVC practitioner in Tel Aviv'), while general promotion addresses topics with no location limit.

For most service businesses and clinics in Israel, local promotion is the most important. The Google Business Profile is the main tool for local promotion, and most business owners miss it or simply don't set it up correctly and in a tailored way.

What is a Google Business Profile?

The business profile on Google that appears in maps and local search results - a free and powerful tool, essential for any business serving a local audience.

The profile includes the business name, address, phone, opening hours, client reviews and photos. A business with an up-to-date profile and at least 10 positive reviews is seen as far more trustworthy and professional than one with no such presence. Setting up the profile takes about an hour, and its contribution lasts for years.

Is it important to collect Google reviews?

Definitely. Client reviews are a critical ranking factor on Google, and about 90% of people read reviews and recommendations before choosing to contact a business.

Asking satisfied clients for reviews is one of the simplest and most rewarding actions for growing the business. Sending a direct link to the Google review page the moment a client expresses satisfaction is simple and convenient for them, and makes a huge difference to your placement in local results.

What is a digital strategy for a business?

A work plan that defines how the business is represented online, which channels are right to use, what the key messages are, and how to turn casual visitors into paying clients.

A good strategy always starts with understanding the target audience and defining what they're looking for. Without a clear strategy, business owners tend to choose platforms and tools just because "everyone does it," which usually leads to wasting resources, time and money on channels that don't suit them.

Is it worth buying a .co.il domain?

For Israeli businesses addressing a local audience in Israel, the answer is yes. A domain with an Israeli extension signals relevance to the local market to Google and helps ranking.

The cost of an Israeli domain is very low (about 30-60 shekels a year). It's recommended to also buy the parallel .com version and point it to the main .co.il domain, to keep others from grabbing it. A domain bought early accumulates age and SEO authority over time, so it's worth doing right at the start.

How much traffic does a small business website need?

If about 10% of visitors get in touch and your goal is 5 inquiries (leads) a month, you'll need about 50 visitors. For most service businesses, 100-200 unique visitors a month is definitely enough to start.

The amount of traffic isn't everything; the quality of visitors matters far more. 50 visitors who came to the website after searching Google for exactly the specific service you offer are far better than 500 visitors who arrived by mistake or from a search that was too general. Focused SEO draws a more precise and ready audience to you.

What is link building?

The process of building links from other websites that point to yours, which Google interprets as recommendations that increase the website's authority.

For local businesses, links from business directories and professional directories in Israel help ranking a lot. Writing guest articles on relevant websites, taking part in podcasts that link to your website, and registering with leading directories are natural and effective ways to build a strong, quality link profile.

Can you build an online presence without a budget?

Only partially. You can set up a basic Wix website, open a Google Business Profile and be active on one social network at almost no monetary cost.

However, organic promotion (SEO) demands an enormous investment of time. If you don't have the free time to invest in it, you'll need to put budget into professionals or quality content writing. The no-budget route demands much greater personal time resources and usually yields results at a slower pace. The question is always what you have more of to invest - time or money.

What is inbound marketing?

A marketing strategy that draws clients to the business naturally through value, instead of approaching them actively or interrupting them with ads.

SEO, blog writing and creating content that answers audience questions are excellent examples. For most service businesses, inbound marketing works best in the long run, because the visitor who reaches you has already decided they need a solution, and is only looking for the right person to turn to (for example, someone searching for 'NVC practitioner in Haifa').

What's the difference between direct, organic, and referral traffic?

Direct: visitors who typed the website address straight into the browser. Organic: visitors who came from the free search results on Google. Referral: visitors who came through a link on another website.

Each of these traffic types points to a different marketing channel and helps you understand what works best for you. Organic traffic tends to grow gradually over time, while direct traffic points to an audience that already knows you well. Using Google Analytics lets you analyze this data precisely and focus your efforts.

Is it worth trying TikTok for a spiritual business?

It depends mainly on your target audience. TikTok is great for creating fast, wide exposure among a young audience, while for an audience aged 35 and over, Facebook and Instagram may be more relevant and effective.

The guiding rule is that one platform managed exceptionally well is far better than ten platforms maintained at a mediocre level. TikTok demands consistent investment and significant time resources to create short videos (at least 3-4 times a week), and it's worth making sure you have the ability and the desire to invest in it before starting.
